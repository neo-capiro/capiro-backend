# Independent review of the four-table TRUNCATE repair

**Disposition: no unresolved finding in this narrow repair under the documented hardened runtime-role posture.** The missing TRUNCATE revocation at PR27 checkpoint `9a151f4dbf9e910ce9a78c3cb40a5dc53390508e` is resolved for migration SHA-256 `40a37eb33562beb70fb6195738822361d640241fc35af3fd2632421e7688413a`. This review covers the two changed REVOKEs, their comments, and the native evidence below. It does not extend the prior feature review or approve deployment, business policy, or a deployed role topology. The reviewer authored none of this SQL repair or these native ACL fixtures and ran no database or role mutations during this review.

The [migration](../../apps/api/prisma/migrations/20281015040000_engagement_plans/migration.sql) now revokes `DELETE,TRUNCATE` from `capiro_app` on plans/waves and `UPDATE,DELETE,TRUNCATE` on command receipts/revisions. The four-table coverage is complete. The existing allowed writes, RLS policies, immutable-history triggers, and cleanup FKs are unchanged. The original [DELETE-only tail](https://github.com/neo-capiro/capiro-backend/blob/9a151f4dbf9e910ce9a78c3cb40a5dc53390508e/apps/api/prisma/migrations/20281015040000_engagement_plans/migration.sql#L236-L246) left `D` from the default ACL intact. PostgreSQL identifies `D` as TRUNCATE; RLS excludes whole-table TRUNCATE, and TRUNCATE does not fire DELETE triggers. [Privilege definitions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/ddl-priv.html), [RLS behavior](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/ddl-rowsecurity.html), [TRUNCATE behavior](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/sql-truncate.html).

The frozen [hardening runbook, lines 60–75](https://github.com/neo-capiro/capiro-backend/blob/cd3c51e940f14e1b2a1040b0ffa445e85d68b332/scripts/sql/2026-07-19-rls-role-hardening.sql#L60-L75) documents full `arwdDxt` direct grants, removes app membership in master, then grants app to master in the reverse direction. That reverse grant gives the app no master privileges. The [remediation record, §6.2–6.4](https://github.com/neo-capiro/capiro-backend/blob/cd3c51e940f14e1b2a1040b0ffa445e85d68b332/docs/plans/2026-07-19-capiro-app-rls-bypass-remediation.md#L70-L112) records dev execution and scratch-role behavior; its opening status keeps production separately gated. Neither the older PrismaService comment nor this scratch proof establishes a current production posture.

The final regression also proves the restriction's boundary: owner inheritance, owner SET-only access, parent-role TRUNCATE inheritance, parent SET-only access, and PUBLIC TRUNCATE each still allow truncation after a direct app revoke. All five are classified as unsupported by the fixture qualification predicate; this is not an inline migration rejection. The final receipt derives `acceptedUnsafeProfiles = 0` from those results and verifies cleanup restored its supported fixture posture. The original 27-check before result (12 passed, 15 failed), its executed script, and the earlier after iterations remain preserved separately. Destructive diagnostics roll back. The native runs used PostgreSQL 16.14 and dedicated databases on the owned loopback cluster; the controlled three-role probe is not a general target-preflight implementation.

Direct revocation cannot negate privileges from PUBLIC or other roles, and ownership retains regrant power even when ordinary owner privileges were revoked. A future authorized target preflight must inspect actual ownership, effective table ACLs, inherited roles, and every SET-able role's effective privileges and owner reachability. `has_table_privilege(app, table, 'TRUNCATE')` alone misses SET-only paths. [REVOKE semantics](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/sql-revoke.html), [role inheritance and SET](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/role-membership.html), [privilege inquiry functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/functions-info.html).

The frozen hardening runbook's blanket `GRANT ALL ON ALL TABLES` would reintroduce TRUNCATE and DELETE on these new tables if replayed later. Effective ACL qualification must follow any such operation. No role, PUBLIC/default grant, or frozen runbook is changed by this repair. An additional migration topology assertion was not required by the existing contract and is not introduced here; unsupported posture remains an explicit adoption precondition. No deployed target preflight was executed.
