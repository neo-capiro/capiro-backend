The proposed reconciliation preserves the actual operator interface and confines its source-policy exception to the current reviewed snapshot trio. Only those arms may be excluded from the resolve detector. Required refusal controls are finite: missing, duplicate, extra or spoofed snapshot paths; changed raw envelopes/comments and unsupported normalization; nested or duplicated operator-looking arms; changed case selector, shift, empty-name check, composition, log, schema, posture or quoted argument; extra shell tokens, heredoc/continuation tricks and fallthrough terminators; resolve outside the two arms; history-table or rollback mutation anywhere; and attempted exceptions on unknown scripts. Positive controls are the exact LF trio, equivalent CRLF bytes and generic safe deployment source. Repeated calls must not retain regex, input-array or prior-result state that changes later findings.

Scope and operational risks remain explicit. A caller supplies source snapshots, so a matching digest attests the supplied bytes, not a deployed file, image or caller identity. Literal fingerprints must not be recomputed from an untrusted input, configurable through environment or delegated to a caller-provided approval flag. Comments about tickets do not authenticate a ticket or approver. Static selection does not prevent two operators from racing, freeze deployment traffic, validate the database's partial DDL/DML state or prove whether applied versus rolled-back is correct. The existing controlled-recovery procedure owns those checks; no runtime authorization or serialization is added by this source-only slice.

The shell entrypoints, dispatchers, database URL helper, actual schema/migration history, replay/validate/generate rules and unrelated promotion/vendor guards are preservation anchors. A owns the pure guard, its tests, Phase A alignment and exact runbooks. Root owns the raw lint operand, metadata/smoke and consolidated handoff.
