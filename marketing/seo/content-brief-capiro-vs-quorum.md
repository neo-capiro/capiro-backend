## Why this page

Quorum shows up in nearly every "best government affairs software" roundup (Pursuit, LegiStorm, ZipDo) and owns the "public affairs software" and "legislative tracking" search terms. Capiro currently has zero visibility for any "alternative to X" or "vs." query, which is exactly where switching-stage buyers search. This page is one of the fastest ways to intercept that demand once the site can support real URLs.

## Positioning angle

Don't attack Quorum's data quality or coverage — it's well-regarded there. The honest, defensible wedge is **breadth-of-suite vs. depth-of-action**: Quorum is a strong tracking-and-monitoring platform; Capiro's differentiation (per its own site) is an AI Chief of Staff, Meri, that goes beyond surfacing information and actually drafts outreach, manages every client objective as a written ask, and automates the day-to-day production work. Frame it as "Quorum tells you what's happening. Capiro helps you act on it" — which mirrors Capiro's existing homepage copy ("Others provide intelligence. Capiro helps you act on it.").

## Sourced facts to build the comparison on

Pulled from public G2/Capterra/Software Advice reviews (verify current at time of publishing, and cite or link where used):

- Quorum bundles 15+ products across federal, state, grassroots, PAC, and international use cases — genuinely comprehensive, but reviewers note the breadth can make the interface "clunky" and harder to learn for infrequent users.
- Quorum pricing is quote-based across nine separate plan tiers (Federal, State, Grassroots, PAC, Local, School Board, Stakeholder, International, Professional Services) — no public pricing, and packages can require piecing together multiple tiers depending on scope.
- Reviewers consistently cite strong state legislative tracking and responsive customer service as strengths.
- Some reviewers note historical data reliability/searchability issues when rechecking older votes or amendments.

Do not fabricate weaknesses beyond what's sourced above — link to or footnote the review source if a specific claim is used, since this is a competitor-facing page.

## Recommended structure

1. **H1:** Capiro vs. Quorum
2. **Intro (2–3 sentences):** Who evaluates this comparison and why; one-line summary of the core difference (tracking vs. acting).
3. **At-a-glance comparison table** (see below)
4. **"Where Quorum is strong"** — a short, fair section (breadth of coverage, state tracking, established player). Builds credibility and avoids sounding defensive.
5. **"Where Capiro is different"** — Meri as an AI Chief of Staff: drafts outreach automatically, turns client objectives into written asks, single view across every client/capability, works with existing tools rather than replacing them.
6. **Pricing framing** — Capiro should state its own pricing model plainly (even "contact us" is fine) and note Quorum's quote-based, multi-tier structure as a contrast in simplicity, not a knock.
7. **FAQ block** (targets "People Also Ask" and supports FAQ schema): "Is Capiro a Quorum alternative?", "Does Capiro do legislative tracking?", "What does Capiro do that Quorum doesn't?", "How much does Capiro cost compared to Quorum?"
8. **CTA:** Demo request (reuse existing `openModal()` form).

## Tone & brand guardrails

- Every competitor claim should be either sourced (link to a review site) or removed.
- Have legal/marketing sign off before publishing, standard practice for named-competitor pages.
